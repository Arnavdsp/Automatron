# Common due-diligence red flags

Summary written for this project. Not legal advice.

## Title and encumbrance

An unreleased mortgage or deed of trust from a prior owner. A mechanic's lien, particularly
where recent work is visible. A judgment lien against a party in the chain. Access that depends
on an easement across someone else's land, especially an unrecorded or prescriptive one.

## Survey and physical

A structure or fence crossing a boundary in either direction. An improvement inside a required
setback. An encroachment by a neighbour that has persisted long enough to raise adverse
possession or prescriptive easement questions in some jurisdictions.

## Use restrictions

Association covenants restricting accessory structures, exterior changes, rentals or business
use. A restriction that conflicts with the buyer's stated intended use is the highest-value
finding a document review can produce, because it is specific and actionable.

## Lease matters, for tenanted property

Below-market rent with a long remaining term. Renewal options at fixed rents. Assignment
provisions that survive a sale. Rights of first refusal. The absence of estoppel certificates
confirming what the tenants believe their terms to be.

## Environmental

A prior industrial or fuel-storage use. An adjoining site with a known release. A phase one
report recommending further investigation, which is a finding in itself.

## Inspection

Roof, structure, water intrusion and major systems at or past expected service life. A finding
of active water intrusion generally outranks cosmetic items.

## How severity is assigned

Severity should come from a documented rubric rather than the reviewer's impression, so two
reviewers reach the same ranking. An unreleased lien and an easement crossing the planned build
area are high because they block or defeat the buyer's purpose. A minor inspection item is low.
