# Dispute mediation practices overview

Summary written for this project. Not legal advice.

## Why a neutral summary helps

Most tenant and association disputes are factual disagreements wrapped in frustration. Setting
out a dated timeline, each party's stated position, the clauses each relies on, and the facts
that are actually missing often narrows the dispute before anyone reaches a decision.

## Structure that works

A timeline from the correspondence, with dates and sources. Each party's position in their own
terms, without characterising it. The governing clauses with page references. What is disputed
as opposed to what is merely disagreeable. What information would resolve the factual questions.

## When mediation is not the answer

Matters heading toward eviction belong with counsel and the court, because only an authorised
body can order possession. Threats of violence, and language suggesting discrimination on a
protected characteristic, should be escalated rather than mediated. Where a party is already
represented, communication normally runs through counsel.

## Wording

Summaries describe positions and evidence. They do not find fault, state who will win, or advise
a party to take a legal step. The options offered are process options: mediation, a written
notice, a request for documentation, a referral to counsel.
