# Utility easement crossing a planned garage

Synthetic case. Details are fictional.

**Situation.** A buyer under contract on a suburban lot intended to build a detached garage in
the rear yard.

**What the review showed.** Schedule B-II of the title commitment carried a ten foot utility
easement along the rear boundary. The survey placed the easement across the area shown for the
garage on the buyer's sketch. Separately, the association covenants required written
architectural committee approval for any accessory structure, with a 45 day decision period.

**What the attorney decided.** The findings were carried into a red-flag memo naming the
document and page for each, with questions about whether the easement holder would consent to
relocation and whether the build area could shift forward while still meeting setbacks. The memo
did not state whether the plan was feasible.

**Lesson.** The highest-value finding was the one tied directly to the buyer's stated intended
use, which is what made it specific enough to act on.
