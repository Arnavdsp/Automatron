# Counterfeit and IP complaint handling overview

Summary written for this project. Verify against the applicable law and platform rules.

## Where complaints originate

Most enforcement begins with a notice from a rights holder alleging that a listing infringes a
trademark, a copyright or a design right, or that goods are counterfeit. Platforms generally
act on the notice first and offer the seller an appeal, because the alternative is to adjudicate
rights they cannot verify.

## Why a complaint is not a finding

A notice asserts a claim. It may be well founded, mistaken about the specific listing, or filed
in a dispute between parties that both have some right to sell. Parallel imports, authorised
resale and exhaustion doctrines differ by jurisdiction and are frequently the substance of a
genuine dispute.

## What distinguishes a strong appeal

Documentary chain from an authorised source to the units sold. Quantities that reconcile. Dates
that precede the sales. A named supplier that can be checked. Where a rights holder has retracted
a notice, the retraction itself.

## Wording

Findings should describe what the evidence shows and where it falls short. A reviewer decides
the outcome. Statements that a seller is guilty or innocent, or that goods are counterfeit,
exceed what this analysis can support.
