# FCC Part 25, satellite communications

Summary written for this project. Verify against the official source before relying on it.

Part 25 governs authorisation of satellite communications for systems licensed by the United
States, covering space stations and the earth stations that communicate with them.

## Authorisations

A space station authorisation covers the satellite or constellation itself: the orbit, the
frequencies, the emission characteristics and the service. Earth station authorisations cover
the ground segment, including transmitting earth stations and, in some cases, blanket
authorisation for large numbers of identical terminals.

Small satellites meeting specific eligibility criteria on lifetime, orbit, size and
manoeuvrability may qualify for a streamlined process with a lower fee and a shorter review than
the conventional Part 25 route. Eligibility is narrow and should be checked against the current
rule rather than assumed.

## Technical showings

Applications carry technical exhibits describing the orbit, the frequency plan, power levels
including equivalent isotropically radiated power, antenna patterns, and the interference
analysis showing compatibility with existing and planned systems in the same bands. Spectrum
sharing conditions vary substantially by band.

## Orbital debris mitigation

Part 25 applications include an orbital debris mitigation showing. It addresses collision risk
during and after the mission, the plan for post-mission disposal, casualty risk from any
surviving debris on reentry, and the means by which the operator will accomplish disposal. The
Commission has tightened disposal expectations for low Earth orbit in recent years, and the
current text is the authority.

## Coordination beyond the Commission

A United States licence does not by itself secure international recognition of the frequency
assignment. That comes through filings submitted by the national administration to the
International Telecommunication Union, which is a separate and longer process running in
parallel.

## What this means for a decision brief

A packet brief should distinguish what a domestic authorisation covers from what international
coordination covers, list the technical exhibits required, and flag any band where sharing
conditions are contested. It should never state that spectrum has been secured or that
coordination is complete.
