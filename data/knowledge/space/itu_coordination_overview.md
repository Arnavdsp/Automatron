# ITU frequency coordination for satellite systems

Summary written for this project. Verify against the official source before relying on it.

Radio frequency assignments for satellite systems gain international recognition through the
International Telecommunication Union. Operators do not file directly; a national administration
files on behalf of the operator, which makes the national regulator the first point of contact.

## Sequence

Advance publication information announces a planned system and opens the process.

Coordination request, under Article 9, identifies administrations whose existing or planned
assignments may be affected. Those administrations respond, and bilateral or multilateral
coordination follows. This stage takes the longest and has no guaranteed duration, because it
depends on reaching agreement with each affected party.

Notification records the assignment in the Master International Frequency Register once
coordination is complete, which is what confers international protection.

## Priority

The system is broadly first come, first served by date of filing, tempered by the rules for
particular services and bands. Regulatory deadlines apply: a filing that is not brought into use
within the prescribed period lapses, and milestone requirements apply to non-geostationary
constellations.

## Practical consequences

Coordination timelines are measured in years, not months, and are the usual critical path for a
new system in a contested band. Identifying affected operators early, and opening technical
discussion before formal deadlines, is the main lever an operator has.

## What this means for a decision brief

A brief should name the affected parties it identified and the basis for that finding, describe
the stage the filing has reached, and give indicative rather than committed timelines. Language
suggesting that coordination is complete, that protection is secured, or that a filing has been
accepted is wrong unless a document says so.
